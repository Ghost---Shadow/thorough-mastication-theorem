# Results of the toy runs, 26 Nov 2026

The first 750 runs of [experiment-plan.md](experiment-plan.md) finished on one RTX 4070 laptop card,
with no run diverging. The predictions and the scoring rule were committed before the run, in
[../experiments/PREDICTIONS.md](../experiments/PREDICTIONS.md). Every number below is from
`python experiments/tmt_sgd.py --summarise`, whose full output is in
`experiments/results/summary.txt`. The raw runs are in `experiments/results/`.

**These are toy models on toy data.** They say nothing certain about large models.

## The verdicts

| | Prediction | Verdict | The count behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Per gradient step, with clean labels and new data, no mastication above 1 beats plain SGD | **holds** | no mastication was lower than plain SGD in any seed, in either task |
| P2 | Per example seen, with clean labels and new data, mastication 64 beats plain SGD | **holds** | lower in 5 of 5 seeds for `linear`, and 5 of 5 for `mlp` |
| P3 | With noisy labels and new data, plain SGD beats mastication 64 and `until` | **holds** | lower in 5 of 5 seeds, both tasks, both mastications |
| P4 | Forgetting of the previous batch is larger for mastication 64 than for mastication 1 | **holds** | larger in 5 of 5 seeds in all five clean settings |
| P5 | (a guess) With a fixed pool no mastication above 1 beats plain SGD, and plain SGD wins on noisy digits | **fails** | on clean digits, mastication 4 was lower than plain SGD in 4 of 5 seeds |

**The stop rule was applied, and no verdict changed.** The best rate of plain SGD was at an
end of its list in 6 of the 10 settings. So one rate was added on each side for `linear` (9
and 0.1) and for `mlp` (0.3 and 0.003): 400 more runs, 1,150 in all (the second amendment in
the predictions file). All 200 runs at the two higher rates diverged. The lower rates were
chosen in some settings with noisy labels and lowered a few losses there. One setting (`mlp`,
noisy labels, pool) still has plain SGD's best rate at the lower end; the rule allows one
change, so nothing more was added. `experiments/results/summary.txt` is from all 1,150 runs.
The numbers quoted below did not change.

## What the numbers say

Median test loss at the end of 10,000 gradient steps, clean labels, new data:

| Mastication | Examples seen | `linear` | `mlp` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (plain SGD) | 320,000 | 0.000012 | 0.00116 |
| 4 | 80,000 | 0.000112 | 0.00163 |
| 16 | 20,000 | 0.00539 | 0.00216 |
| 64 | 5,024 | 0.0967 | 0.00275 |
| `until` | 16,858 and 1,843 | 0.00925 | 0.00416 |

- **Per step, masticating loses, and by a lot.** For the same 10,000 steps, plain SGD ends far
  lower than every mastication. For `linear` the gap to masticate 64 is four orders of magnitude.
- **Per example, masticating wins.** After the 5,024 examples that mastication 64 sees, plain SGD has a
  median test loss of 0.234 on `linear` and 0.0039 on `mlp`. Mastication 64 has 0.097 and 0.0028 from
  the same examples. It pays for that with 64 times the gradient steps.
- **Noise hurts masticating more.** With noisy labels and new data, plain SGD ends at 0.012 on
  `linear`; mastication 64 ends at 0.21 and `until` at 0.23.
- **Forgetting grows with the mastication.** The mean rise in the loss on the previous batch goes
  from 0.0008 (mastication 1) to 0.043 (mastication 64) on `linear`, and from 0.0002 to 0.030 on `digits`.
- **`until` is too strict for the network.** Reaching 1% of the arrival loss took 19 steps a
  batch on `linear`, and 174 to 250 on `mlp`, close to the cap of 256.

## The one surprise

On the clean digits, with a fixed pool gone through many times, mastication 4 ended with a lower test
loss than plain SGD in 4 of 5 seeds: a median of 0.1375 against 0.1408, with the same median
test accuracy of 96.4%. Mastication 16 was lower in 3 of 5 seeds and mastication 64 in 2 of 5. This is why
P5 fails, and it fails in the idea's favour. The gap is about 2% of the loss, the setting is
one small dataset, and P5 was a guess, so this is a thing to test again and not a result to
build on.

## A check that was not in the plan

P2 as registered picks each method's learning rate by its loss at the end of the run. For
plain SGD the comparison point is early in its run (step 157), where another rate might do
better. So I also gave each method its best rate of the three at the comparison point. Mastication
64 is still lower: in 5 of 5 seeds for `linear` (medians 0.097 and 0.234) and in 4 of 5 for
`mlp` (0.0026 and 0.0032). This check was thought of after the results were seen.

## What this means for the idea

- The theorems' reading held on every count they made a prediction about: masticating is the
  better use of each batch, and the worse use of each gradient step.
- So TMT-SGD is worth it only where loading a batch costs much more than a step. Plain SGD
  reached the test loss that mastication 64 ended with after 750 steps on `linear` (at most 4.8 times
  the examples mastication 64 saw) and after a median of 360 steps on `mlp` (2.3 times the examples,
  from 0.3 to 6.4 across the seeds). Mastication 64 took 10,000 steps for it. Counting both costs,
  masticating comes out cheaper only if loading one batch costs more than about 16 gradient steps
  on `linear` and about 47 on `mlp`. The log is coarse here (one row every 250 steps), so these
  are rough. (An earlier version of this page said "2.4 batches". That number was the ratio of
  two losses, not of two batch counts, and it was wrong.)
- With noisy labels it is worse on both counts that were measured.
- The digits result is the one hint that a short mastication can help per step on a small fixed
  dataset. It is the place to look next.

## How it was run

Five processes shared the card (`--worker 0/5` to `4/5`), which does not change any run. The
sum of the run times is 176 minutes; with five workers the wall-clock time was under an hour.
